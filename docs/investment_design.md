# Purposeful investment — proposed economic specification

Status: earlier project-specific proposal, now deferred following the requirement
to use named textbook models. Not implemented in the economic engine.

Start with [the textbook foundation](textbook_foundation.md). The equations and
examples below remain a record of the earlier proposal; they are not the selected
textbook specification. In particular, the three-period horizon, constant-price
forecast, capped dividends and runoff continuation do not together constitute
an established textbook model.

This is the next economic milestone in the roadmap (previously called 1.2),
within the same Tiny Economy app. It is separate from the software's saved-file
and engine version identifiers.

## Recommendation

Let firms choose hiring and investment together to maximize forecast discounted
dividends to their owners. Keep the current percentage policy as a benchmark.
Use a short planning horizon, recalculate every period, and state the forecasting
and valuation assumptions explicitly. Households retain their current choices
until the following household-saving milestone.

The first prototype should use three planned production periods and a common
discount factor of 0.95 per period. These are proposed teaching defaults, not
estimated parameters. A period has no calendar length, and the discount factor
is not an interest rate paid on money or loans.

## Decisions and information

- Each firm takes the current goods price and wage as given. In evaluating a
  candidate current market price/wage, it forecasts those same prices and wages
  for future periods. Productivity and depreciation also stay constant.
- This is a disclosed constant-price forecast. Future market clearing may
  produce different outcomes; firms do not know the realized future or manipulate
  prices by anticipating their own market power.
- At each real period, execute only the first hiring/investment decision, settle
  the accounts, observe the result and replan. Forecasts never enter the ledger.
- Preserve dividend timing: previously earned eligible profit is distributed
  before current wages. Preserve each firm's original operating-float rule.
- Investment retains some of the firm's own output. It reduces sales revenue;
  it is not a cash payment to itself. New capital operates next period.
- Keep the common market, fixed household ownership shares and funded settlement.
  There is no lending, capital resale, inventory accumulation or external shock.

Hiring cannot be left at its current static optimum while calling the combined
decision optimal. Labor produces both saleable output and investible goods, and
its payroll uses cash that may be valuable in future periods.

## A firm's forecast problem

Normalize forecast money amounts by the current goods price `p`. Write:

| Symbol | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `K` | Opening productive capital |
| `b` | Cash after the opening dividend, divided by `p` |
| `u = w/p` | Real wage per unit of labor |
| `f = original_float/p` | Protected operating float in the same units |
| `A`, `delta` | Productivity and depreciation |
| `L`, `I` | Chosen labor and own-output investment |
| `beta`, `H` | Discount factor and number of planned production periods |

For each forecast production period:

```text
Q = A sqrt(K L)
0 <= u L <= b                 payroll must be funded before sales
0 <= I <= Q                  invest only produced goods

K_next = (1 - delta) K + I
cash_close = b - u L + Q - I
profit = Q - u L - delta K
dividend_next = min(max(profit, 0), max(cash_close - f, 0))
b_next = cash_close - dividend_next
```

The bounds already imply nonnegative closing cash; an extra discretionary cash
buffer is unnecessary. Both cash and capital are state variables. A firm with
zero payroll cash can be dormant; numerical code must distinguish this from an
economy with no feasible aggregate clearing outcome.

The objective is:

```text
V_0(K, b) = R(K, b)
V_h(K, b) = max over feasible (L, I):
    beta * [dividend_next + V_(h-1)(K_next, b_next)]
```

The dividend at the current opening has already been paid and is constant with
respect to these decisions. `R` is the continuation rule below. Do not add
operating profit or closing book equity to the dividend objective: that would
count retained value again. Replacement-cost equity remains an accounting
measure, separate from the forecast value of owner payouts.

## Value beyond the planning horizon

Use a feasible reference policy after the last planned period: make no further
investment and hire each subsequent period to maximize current gross surplus
subject to payroll cash. Keep the same price/wage forecast and dividend rule.

```text
I = 0
L = min(A^2 K / (4 u^2), b/u)
```

`R(K,b)` is the discounted sum of dividends generated by this continuation.
This gives surviving productive capital value through its earnings, without
inventing a liquidation transaction. Cash that the dividend rule would retain
forever receives no fictitious terminal payout. The rule is a feasible reference,
not a claim of globally optimal continuation or true market value.

The present profit cap can leave cash permanently inside the firm. Replacing
worn capital may preserve dividends while using cash that otherwise remains
undistributable. This is a substantive consequence of retaining that rule, not
a general prediction about real firms. The continuation is hypothetical and is
reconsidered after every actual period; it does not make the economy stop growing
after three periods.

The tail can be evaluated with a stated error bound. Let `gamma = A^2/(4u)`.
Current gross surplus is at most `gamma*K`, and with no investment capital
falls by the factor `1-delta` each period. After simulating `N` tail periods,
the remaining discounted dividends are bounded by:

```text
beta^(N+1) * gamma * K_N / [1 - beta*(1-delta)]
```

Require `0 < beta < 1`; the bound also covers zero depreciation. Stop when the
bound meets the declared absolute and relative value tolerances, or return a
clear unresolved-value diagnostic. A runtime limit must never silently stand
in for convergence.

## Worked examples

These examples compare three feasible plans for one firm at given forecast
prices; they are not two-firm market-clearing results or a proof of joint
optimality. Each plan uses funded static hiring for illustration during the
first three periods, then the same runoff continuation. The future optimizer
must search jointly over hiring and investment, and can find better plans.

All cases use `p=w=1`, opening capital `K=10`, depreciation `delta=0.10`,
discount factor `beta=0.95`, and three planned production periods. Initial
post-dividend cash equals the protected float. The three plans are:

- **Zero:** retain no output as investment.
- **Replace:** retain `min(delta*K, Q)` each period.
- **Retain surplus:** retain `max(0, Q-u*L)` each period.

Forecast discounted dividends, in units of X:

| Case | Productivity A | Opening cash / float | Zero | Replace | Retain surplus | Best of these plans |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Cash-poor firm | 1.0 | 0.1 | **8.175254** | 0.000000 | 6.815014 | Zero investment |
| Replacement case | 0.7 | 10.0 | 1.474138 | **1.873611** | 1.351135 | Replacement |
| Expansion case | 1.2 | 1.0 | 16.135140 | 18.697369 | **21.177419** | Expansion |

Their first decisions and funded outcomes are:

| Best illustrated plan | Labor | Output X | Investment X | Next capital | Closing cash before next dividend | Next dividend |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Zero investment | 0.100000 | 1.000000 | 0.000000 | 9.000000 | 1.000000 | 0.000000 |
| Replacement | 1.225000 | 2.450000 | 1.000000 | 10.000000 | 10.225000 | 0.225000 |
| Expansion | 1.000000 | 3.794733 | 2.794733 | 11.794733 | 1.000000 | 0.000000 |

In the cash-poor case, trying to replace all worn capital retains the entire
first output and leaves no payroll money; production then stops. Selling that
output instead builds cash for future production. In the replacement case,
capital stays at 10 and the firm pays 0.225 each following opening. The expansion
plan preserves payroll cash while increasing capital, with dividends deferred.

Reproduce with `python docs/investment_examples.py`. The script imports no app
or engine module, performs no writes, and bounds each reported value's omitted
dividend tail below `1e-10`. Its numbers are illustrations of the proposed rules,
not a production investment solver.

## Compatibility with household saving

For this milestone, the common firm discount factor is an explicit managerial
objective. It is not derived from the currently myopic, heterogeneous households
and is not a claim of household welfare maximization.

The household-saving design must specify how households value future consumption,
leisure, liquidity and dividends, and how owners agree on firms' objectives.
Heterogeneous preferences need not imply one common firm discount factor.
Keep price/wage forecasts, dividend timing and forecast-versus-realized records
compatible now; settle ownership aggregation before claiming a unified dynamic
equilibrium later. Do not introduce a loan interest rate to stand in for patience.

## Product and accounting

Offer **Percentage policy** and **Forward-looking investment** within the same
workspace. Start with a common planning horizon and discount factor, with advanced
controls only after their sensitivity has been demonstrated. Each firm may use
either policy; the benchmark retains its existing economic behavior.

Show actual investment in X, replacement needs, capital change and cash available
for payroll. A compact history should connect these with household consumption.
An investment explanation should show the selected plan's forecast owner value,
the value of a feasible zero-investment alternative and any funding constraint.
Label forecasts and realized values separately. A higher forecast firm value is
not automatically better for every household.

The percentage benchmark's investment cannot produce an operating cash loss
under its existing rules. Purposeful retention may reduce cash, so its new
cash-flow outcomes must be explained and certified explicitly. Investment is
still a physical event, and every actual money payment still has a counterparty.

## Implementation gates

1. Prototype the single-firm problem first. Check small cases against exhaustive
   feasible searches, refine the search, verify the tail bound and examine
   sensitivity to planning horizon and discount factor. Include zero investment,
   replacement, expansion, binding cash, zero cash and zero depreciation.
2. Validate runtime before promising interactive controls. A finite action grid
   is useful as an independent benchmark, but cannot establish an exact continuous
   optimum and can introduce artificial jumps in market demand.
3. Integrate a general goods-and-labor clearing solver with certified firm choices.
   The existing analytic zero-target path relies on percentage-policy formulas;
   it cannot be reused unaltered for purposeful firms. Retain it for benchmark-only
   runs. Report ambiguity or failure explicitly and preserve completed history.
4. Test money conservation, physical resources, payroll funding, dividends and
   accounting independently. Keep material error rejection; do not weaken
   certificates just to accept a planned trajectory.
5. Preserve benchmark results and add forecast records to portable experiments.
   Choose a new engine/file compatibility policy explicitly before release; never
   silently reinterpret a previously saved experiment under new decision rules.
6. Review mobile controls and explanations only after the economic and numerical
   prototype passes these gates. Deploy the reliability fixes separately from
   this new economic behavior.

## Reassessment before changing the model

The textbook requirement supersedes this package as the implementation plan.
The isolated optimal-growth reference now checks a named model against its known
solution. The next firm specification must start from neoclassical investment,
state financing and ownership assumptions, and identify every extension needed
by Tiny Economy. Do not integrate this earlier runoff proposal by default.

## Method reference

Discounted dynamic programming supplies the framework for choosing actions that
affect future state. The specific cash, dividend and forecast rules above are
proposed Tiny Economy assumptions, not results guaranteed by that framework.
See [QuantEcon: Discrete State Dynamic Programming](https://python-advanced.quantecon.org/discrete_dp.html).
